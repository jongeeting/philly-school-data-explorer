# Composite feasibility: first pass (Oct 3, 2026)

Scope: Philadelphia district and brick-and-mortar charter schools with a unique state code (287 non-criteria schools + 22 criteria schools). Source: PDE Future Ready PA Index data files 2017-18 to 2024-25, PDE School Fast Facts, SDP master school lists 2018-19 to 2025-26. All-students group.

## Data spine
- `crosswalk.csv`: 338 schools/programs keyed on the district's ULCS code, linked to the state key (AUN + school number), NCES, SRC ID, governance, level, admission, council district, GPS.
- The state key is NOT unique for 29 alternative/contracted programs: most share placeholder code 9999 and continuation academies (Stetson, Olney) report under their host school. Their state scores are blended; exclude them from any composite.
- Usable test years: 2018, 2019, 2022-2025. 2020 had no tests (state file repeats 2019); 2021 had low participation and no growth scores; science waived in 2025.

## Results
| Measure | Tracks poverty (r, 2025) | Year-to-year stability (r) | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proficiency, ELA/math | -0.67 to -0.69 (r² about 0.45) | 0.92-0.97 | Stable but mostly a poverty map |
| Growth (PVAAS-based), ELA | -0.22 | 0.17-0.49 single year; 0.41 two-year avg | Fair to poverty, too noisy alone |
| Growth, math | -0.18 | 0.43-0.57; 0.61 two-year avg | Fair, usable with multi-year averaging |
| Regular attendance | -0.54 | 0.81-0.91 | Stable, moderately poverty-linked |
| 4-year graduation | -0.28 | 0.87-0.92 | Only about 85 high schools |

"Beating the odds" (proficiency minus what poverty, EL, and special-ed share predict, within grade band, criteria schools excluded):
- K-8: demographics explain about 55-60% of variation; residuals persist year to year (r 0.48-0.79).
- High school: demographics explain only 20-24%; residuals are very persistent (r about 0.9), which points to selection and enrollment patterns rather than school effect.
- Charter and district K-8 schools average about the same residual (+0.1 vs -0.1 points).
- Top K-8 overperformers include schools in comparatively advantaged catchments (McCall, Greenberg, Jenks), so the state poverty measure undercounts neighborhood advantage. This is the case for census normalization.
